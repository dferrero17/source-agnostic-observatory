Self-Generating, Self-Healing, and Self-Deploying Data Wrappers in Production}

\begin{abstract}
% DRAFT abstract -- tighten once the results section is locked.
A smart city observatory is only as useful as the breadth of sources it can
ingest, yet most indicator platforms hard-wire a handful of feeds and pay an
engineer to maintain a wrapper for each. We present \sys{}, a production
smart city observatory whose ingestion layer is source-agnostic: any REST API,
spreadsheet, or CSV export becomes a normalized indicator time series with no
source-specific code written by a human. Each source is handled by a data
wrapper that we treat as an autonomic artifact with three properties. It is
\emph{self-generating}: a large language model fills a fixed extraction template
from source metadata and a data sample, probing live endpoints before
committing to extraction logic. It is \emph{self-healing}: a monitor re-invokes
the model with the crash traceback and repairs the wrapper in place. And it is
\emph{self-deploying}: wrappers are launched, supervised, checkpointed, and
relaunched as isolated processes with no operator in the loop. We frame the
three properties as a single autonomic manager over the generated artifact and
report a \deploymentDays{}-day production deployment: \nWrappers{} wrappers
across API, XLSX, and CSV sources ingested \nDataPoints{} data points;
\nRetried{} wrappers (\pctRetried\%) crashed and entered the repair loop, after
which 42 of 43 crashed no further at a median of \healLatencyMedian{}\,s per
repair; and the whole platform runs in roughly 1.5\,GiB of memory. The boundary
of the approach is sharp: the loops absorb heterogeneity and code defects, while
quota, credential, and configuration failures stay outside their reach.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Frame: the ingestion bottleneck is what limits the breadth of a smart-city
% observatory; source-agnosticism is the property worth engineering for.
Smart city and destination observatories promise a single pane of glass over
heterogeneous urban indicators: air and water quality, mobility, energy,
overnight stays, cultural participation. The promise is bounded in practice by
the ingestion layer. Each indicator is published by a different statistical
office, municipality, or sector association, as a REST API, an Excel workbook,
or an ad hoc CSV export, with its own schema, date format, language, and failure
modes. The classical answer is one extraction wrapper per source, but writing
and maintaining those wrappers is the cost that has limited mediator-based data
integration since the
1990s~\cite{wiederhold1992mediators,kushmerick2000wrapper,lerman2003wrapper}.
A platform that can only afford a fixed set of wrappers is, in effect, not an
observatory of its city but of the few sources someone had time to integrate.

We call an ingestion layer \emph{source-agnostic} when adding a new source costs
no source-specific engineering: a domain user registers the source through a web
form, and the platform produces, runs, and maintains the wrapper on its own.
This paper describes \sys{} (anonymized for review), a production smart city
observatory for a European region in which source-agnostic ingestion is
realized by treating each wrapper as an \emph{autonomic} artifact in the sense
of Kephart and Chess~\cite{kephart2003vision}: a self-managing component with a
closed control loop over its own lifecycle.

Concretely, every wrapper has three self-* properties.
\emph{Self-generating}: when a source is registered, a large language model
(LLM) fills a fixed extraction template from the indicator metadata and a sample
of the data, and for live APIs it probes the endpoint through a controlled fetch
tool before writing code (Section~\ref{sec:gen}).
\emph{Self-healing}: generated code fails in production, and a monitor re-invokes
the model with the failing source and its captured stack trace, redeploying the
repaired wrapper automatically (Section~\ref{sec:heal}).
\emph{Self-deploying}: wrappers run as isolated operating-system processes that
the platform spawns, supervises, checkpoints, and relaunches without an operator,
including resume-from-watermark after restarts and automatic image updates in
production (Section~\ref{sec:deploy}).

This paper makes three contributions.
First, we give a source-agnostic observatory architecture and the contract that
makes it work: any source is reduced to a stream of normalized
$(\textrm{timestamp}, \textrm{value})$ points by a generated wrapper that the
platform owns end to end (Section~\ref{sec:arch}).
Second, we formalize the wrapper as a single autonomic manager and separate its
three self-* loops, relating each to the relevant line of prior work in
autonomic computing, LLM code generation and repair, and wrapper maintenance
(Section~\ref{sec:selfstar}).
Third, we report measurements from a real \deploymentDays{}-day deployment
rather than a benchmark, including a natural pre-repair baseline and a failure
taxonomy that marks exactly where the autonomic loops stop helping
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). \coderepo

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

% Four threads; keep each to a tight paragraph. Reuse refs.bib keys already
% present from the prior study.

\subsection{Smart city and tourism observatories}
Smart tourism research calls for destination-level data
infrastructures~\cite{gretzel2015smart,buhalis2013smart}, indicator schemes such
as ETIS define what such platforms should
measure~\cite{ec2016etis}, and implementation studies report uneven
adoption~\cite{modica2018assessment} and warehouse- or survey-based
dashboards~\cite{fuchs2014big}. Studies of smart destination programs repeatedly
identify data availability for sustained indicator monitoring as the
bottleneck~\cite{ivarsbaidal2021measuring,ivarsbaidal2023sustainable}. Reported
platforms rarely document the ingestion layer; we argue that source-agnostic
ingestion is the property that decides how many indicators an observatory can
actually sustain, and we document one in production.

\subsection{Autonomic computing and self-* systems}
The autonomic computing vision frames self-managing components with closed
control loops as the answer to operational
complexity~\cite{kephart2003vision}, with self-configuration, self-healing, and
self-optimization as canonical properties.
% TODO: cite MAPE-K / IBM architectural blueprint here; add to refs.bib.
\sys{} instantiates this vision for data wrappers: generation is
self-configuration of a new artifact, repair is self-healing, and process
supervision is self-deployment. The novelty is the actuator: an LLM that writes
and rewrites the artifact's code, rather than tuning parameters of a fixed one.

\subsection{LLMs for code generation, repair, and extraction}
LLMs generate working programs from natural
language~\cite{chen2021evaluating}, and program repair is increasingly
LLM-based~\cite{xia2023automated}. Feedback loops improve generated code:
Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine}, Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion},
and Self-Debugging~\cite{chen2024selfdebug}, though self-repair gains are not
uniform~\cite{olausson2024selfrepair}. On the data side, Evaporate showed that
generated extraction code amortizes inference cost across
documents~\cite{arora2023evaporate}, AutoScraper generates reusable
scrapers~\cite{huang2024autoscraper}, and prompted~\cite{narayan2022wrangle} and
instruction-tuned~\cite{zhang2024jellyfish} models perform classical data
preprocessing. We differ from generation-time self-repair in the trigger and the
unit: repair fires on a production crash, possibly weeks after generation, and
rewrites the whole wrapper file rather than patching a line.

\subsection{Wrapper induction, maintenance, and ETL automation}
Wrapper induction learned extraction rules from labeled
examples~\cite{kushmerick2000wrapper}; unsupervised systems inferred them from
page regularities~\cite{crescenzi2001roadrunner}; surveys cover the
landscape~\cite{laender2002brief,ferrara2014web}. The maintenance problem,
detecting breakage and re-inducing the wrapper, was recognized
early~\cite{kushmerick2000verification,lerman2003wrapper}. ETL has been modeled
rather than synthesized~\cite{vassiliadis2002conceptual,vassiliadis2009survey},
with automation of schema matching~\cite{rahm2001survey},
curation~\cite{stonebraker2013tamer}, and
wrangling~\cite{furche2016wrangling}. \sys{} replaces both induction and
re-induction with LLM code generation, extending coverage beyond template pages
to arbitrary APIs and spreadsheets, and automates the narrow but recurrent ETL
slice indicator platforms need: single-series extraction into one normalized
time-series schema.

\section{A Source-Agnostic Observatory}
\label{sec:arch}

% Architecture + the source-agnostic contract. Reuse fig_arch.
\Sys{} is a microservice platform: containerized user, knowledge-base,
data-collector, resource, and indicator services (Python, FastAPI) behind an
nginx gateway, plus a React dashboard. Two RabbitMQ brokers separate the control
plane from the data plane; the resource and indicator services each own a
MongoDB store. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the pipeline.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{fig_arch}}
\caption{\Sys{} ingestion pipeline. The resource service generates, deploys, and
repairs wrappers; extracted points flow through two brokers to the indicator
store and the dashboard.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The property that makes ingestion source-agnostic is a narrow contract. A
wrapper, whatever its source, publishes batches of points
$\{(x, y)\}$ with $x$ an ISO~8601 timestamp and $y$ a float; everything upstream
of that contract is generated, and everything downstream is fixed, shared
infrastructure. A domain user registers a source in a web wizard, either
uploading a CSV/XLSX file or configuring an API endpoint with its
authentication, and supplies indicator metadata (name, domain, unit,
periodicity, description). The resource service then owns the wrapper for its
whole lifecycle. The data collector validates each message against the contract,
caches the latest per wrapper, and forwards valid points to the control broker,
where the resource service maps wrapper identifiers to persistent resources and
the indicator service stores, merges, and serves the series.

\section{Self-* Wrappers}
\label{sec:selfstar}

% The theoretical core. Frame all three as one autonomic manager (MAPE-K) over
% the shared wrapper artifact, then take each loop in turn.
We model each wrapper as an autonomic manager whose managed element is the
generated artifact itself: its code, its execution watermarks, and its status.
The manager runs three loops over shared knowledge (Figure~\ref{fig:selfstar}).
The plan-and-execute step of each loop is an LLM call or a process action; the
monitored knowledge is the wrapper's runtime behavior, not a synthetic test
suite. We take the loops in lifecycle order.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{0.92\columnwidth}{!}{\input{fig_selfstar}}
\caption{The autonomic wrapper manager. Three self-* loops act on one shared
artifact; the actuator is an LLM that writes and rewrites code, plus a process
supervisor that deploys it.}
\label{fig:selfstar}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Self-generating}
\label{sec:gen}
% Template-constrained generation + tool-grounded probing. Reuse fig_gen.
Generation is constrained, not free-form. The model fills a fixed wrapper class
with a small number of variable methods (extraction, and for APIs the
date-range fetch and polling interval); transport, batching, resumption, and the
historical-backfill driver are fixed library code the model is told not to
touch. The prompt combines the indicator metadata, the source configuration, a
data sample (first lines of a CSV, rows per sheet of a workbook, or a captured
API response), the required output schema, the template, and a list of hard
rules that encode failure modes seen in development (inclusive date filtering,
consistent timezones, no synthetic data, explicit parsing of non-English dates).
For API sources the generation call exposes a controlled \texttt{fetch\_url}
tool, so the model discovers response nesting, field names, and which date-filter
parameters the endpoint honors from the live endpoint rather than guessing.
Before deployment the code is parsed and scanned for unresolved placeholders,
with one lint retry; there is no pre-flight test, runtime faults are delegated
to self-healing. Figure~\ref{fig:gen} shows the flow.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{\input{fig_gen}}
\caption{Self-generation. The prompt combines metadata, a data sample, the fixed
template, and hard rules; for API sources the model probes the live endpoint
before writing code; output must pass static validation, with one retry, before

\label{fig:gen}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Self-deploying}
\label{sec:deploy}
% This is the property the prior self-healing-focused study underplayed; give it
% room. Process isolation, semaphore, monitor loop, checkpoint-resume, watchtower.
A generated wrapper reaches production with no human action. The resource
service starts each wrapper as a separate operating-system process, with
concurrency bounded by a semaphore and a per-process execution watchdog. File
wrappers run once and exit; API wrappers first backfill history in adaptive
batches, then poll at the interval implied by the indicator periodicity, with no
external scheduler. The service persists two watermarks per wrapper, the oldest
and newest timestamps ever sent, so that after a restart a wrapper resumes from
its watermarks instead of re-extracting, which prevents duplicates and gaps
across deployments. A monitor task polls process states on a fixed period and
records crashes and watchdog terminations. At the platform level, a container
auto-updater pulls new service images in production, so the deployment tracks
upstream without manual rollout. Self-deployment is what lets the other two loops
run unattended: generation hands the manager a process to supervise, and healing
hands it a replacement process to relaunch.

\subsection{Self-healing}
\label{sec:heal}
% Crash-triggered, whole-file repair. Distinguish from generation-time repair.
When the monitor observes a nonzero exit code it marks the wrapper
\texttt{retrying} and starts a repair, unless the wrapper has exhausted its three
attempts, in which case it is marked \texttt{error}. The repair call assembles
the complete crashed source file, the captured stderr trace, and the last stdout
lines, and asks for a complete corrected file that preserves the class structure
and fixes the specific traceback. The reply passes the same static validation as
initial generation, overwrites the wrapper file, increments the retry counter,
and the process is restarted. Two properties distinguish this loop from
generation-time self-repair~\cite{chen2024selfdebug,olausson2024selfrepair}: the
trigger is a production crash that reflects real source behavior, and the unit of
repair is the whole wrapper file, which lets the model restructure extraction
logic rather than patch a single line.

\subsection{Generation and coverage}
\label{sec:res-gen}
The system generated \nWrappers{} wrappers (\nXLSX{} XLSX, \nAPI{} API,
\nCSV{} CSV) across the three domains, and they ingested \nDataPoints{} data
points. Median latency from generation request to first process start was
\genLatencyMedian{}\,s (90th percentile \genLatencyPninety{}\,s), including
queueing, sampling, the model call, and validation. Figure~\ref{fig:lifecycle}

\subsection{Self-healing effectiveness}
\label{sec:res-heal}
\nRetried{} wrappers (\pctRetried\% of all generated) crashed with an unhandled
exception and entered the repair loop, producing 50 repair deployments. A wrapper
is healed if it never crashed again after its final repair: 42 of 43. Median
crash-to-redeploy latency was \healLatencyMedian{}\,s, fully autonomous. The
deployment also contains a natural baseline: before the repair loop was enabled,
36 crashes of the same exit class simply died with no repair. Table~\ref{tab:errors}

\subsection{The autonomic boundary}
\label{sec:res-boundary}
The taxonomy marks where the loops stop helping. Of the \nErrorW{} wrappers in a
terminal \texttt{error} state, almost none reflect defective generated code: 14
never produced code (LLM quota, rate, or availability errors, or a service
restart), and 54 are API pollers killed by a watchdog limit shorter than their
polling interval, a configuration mismatch rather than a code defect. Exactly one
error-status wrapper reflects generated code whose repair never deployed. The
self-* loops absorb source heterogeneity and code defects; quota exhaustion,
credential problems, and misconfigured limits remain operational work.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

% What source-agnosticism buys; threats to validity. Keep honest and short.
Three design choices carry the result: template-constrained generation bounds
what the model must produce, tool-assisted probing grounds extraction in observed
source behavior, and repair feedback is a real production trace. Source-
agnosticism is then an emergent property of the three loops rather than a
separate mechanism: the platform absorbs a new source because it can write,
deploy, and repair that source's wrapper without a human writing code.

Several limitations qualify the measurements. They are observational, from one
deployment, one model family, and one region; there is no contemporaneous control
arm, and the pre-loop period differs in source mix and load. We cannot compare
against hand-written wrappers on the same sources, which were never built.
Recovery counts wrappers restored to a working state, not the semantic
correctness of every extracted value; validation covers schema, not ground truth.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A smart city observatory becomes source-agnostic when its wrappers manage
themselves. \Sys{} shows that LLM-generated wrappers can carry the ingestion
layer of a production indicator platform when each wrapper is self-generating,
self-deploying, and self-healing, and when the three loops share one artifact and
run unattended. In \deploymentDays{} days the system sustained \nWrappers{}
wrappers and \nDataPoints{} points; of \nRetried{} wrappers that crashed, 42 were
left crash-free by at most three repairs, at a median of \healLatencyMedian{}\,s
each and with no operator involvement. Closing the remaining gap calls for
semantic validation of extracted values and for treating environmental failures
as first-class, distinct from code defects.
